Miniproject 2 - Creating a ZCB Term Structure

FRE 6103 Valuation for Financial Engineering, NYU Tandon | Raj Pawar | Methodology

Data and conventions. Wall Street Journal / Tullett Prebon 3 pm quotes for all 353 US Treasury notes and bonds on Sep 4, 2026 (the Week 2 data file). Quotes are clean prices in 32nds with a third decimal in eighths of a 32nd (99.256 = 99 + 25.75/32); we price off the asked side. Regular-way settlement is T+1 business days, Sep 8, 2026 (Monday Sep 7 is Labor Day). Both conventions were verified by recomputing the WSJ asked yield of every bond: median discrepancy 0.02 bp, RMSE 0.5 bp, versus 1.8 bp for the next-best settlement rule and over 86 bp if the quotes are read as decimals.

Cash flows. Each bond pays coupon/2 semi-annually on its maturity day-of-month (month-end maturities pay on month-ends) and 100 at maturity. Accrued interest is actual/actual within the coupon period, coupon/2 x days accrued / days in period, and dirty price = clean price + accrued (slide 34). Time to each payment is t = days/365 from settlement. Street yields use the Treasury convention (semi-annual compounding with a fractional first period, simple interest when one payment remains), which reproduces Excel's YIELD.

Model. The continuously compounded zero rate follows the Svensson (1994) form suggested in the data file, r(t) = b0 + b1 (1 - e^(-t/T1))/(t/T1) + b2 [(1 - e^(-t/T1))/(t/T1) - e^(-t/T1)] + b3 [(1 - e^(-t/T2))/(t/T2) - e^(-t/T2)]. The discount factor is d(t) = e^(-r(t) t) and each bond's model dirty price is the sumproduct of its payment stream with the discount factors, sum_k CF_k d(t_k) (slide 33). The form nests Nelson-Siegel (b3 = 0), is smooth, has finite limits at both ends (r(0) = b0 + b1, r(inf) = b0) and gives the instantaneous forward curve in closed form, f(t) = b0 + b1 e^(-t/T1) + b2 (t/T1) e^(-t/T1) + b3 (t/T2) e^(-t/T2).

Estimation. The six parameters minimise sum_i [(P_i(model) - P_i(market)) / (P_i(market) D_i)]^2, each price error divided by dirty price times modified duration, which is the first-order yield error, so the objective is the sum of squared yield errors (slide 31) while each model price stays a linear sumproduct. Bonds with under 3 months to maturity (14 of 353) are excluded from estimation, as in Gurkaynak, Sack and Wright (2007): their prices reflect money-market conditions and, with near-zero duration, a one-cent quote error is a 50 bp yield error. They are still priced off the curve as an out-of-sample check. Constraints: b0 >= 0, T1, T2 >= 0.05. Because the objective is multimodal in (T1, T2), the least-squares solver is run from 19 starting points on a tau grid; 11 of them reach the same optimum. In Excel the identical objective is the sum-of-squares cell on the Curve sheet, minimised with Solver (GRG Nonlinear); the workbook reproduces every Python model price, yield and zero rate.

Results. b0 = 0.0000 (non-negativity bound active), b1 = 0.0363, b2 = 0.0447, b3 = 0.1622, T1 = 1.76, T2 = 15.93. The curve prices the 339 bonds with a yield RMSE of 2.9 bp (mean absolute 2.1 bp; worst 14.8 bp for the 4.375% 2026-12-15 bond). Nelson-Siegel on the same data gives 7.5 bp, so the second hump is needed. The zero curve rises from 3.63% at the short end to 4.14% at 1 year, 4.49% at 5, 4.79% at 10, 5.33% at 20 and 5.31% at 30 years, peaking at 5.38% around 24 years. The deliverable table lists r(t), d(t) and f(t) for all 245 distinct Treasury payment dates from Sep 15, 2026 to Aug 15, 2056. The asymptotic level b0 is weakly identified by a 30-year sample: pinning it anywhere from 0% to 3% worsens the fit by only 0.3 bp RMSE and moves the in-sample curve by about 10 bp at the two ends (a 3% change in d(30y)), so the payment-date discount factors are robust to it; with b0 = 0 the fitted forward rate decays beyond the sample, so the curve should not be extrapolated past 30 years.
